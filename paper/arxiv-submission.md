### Abstract

The no-three-in-line problem asks how many points of an $n\times n$ grid can be chosen so that no three lie on a straight line. The construction of Hall, Jackson, Sudbery and Wild gives $(3/2-o(1))n$ points for all sufficiently large $n$. To exceed $3n/2$, we build a square ring and flip groups of its points. The flips change which lines are blocked without shrinking the ring, so that many opposite pairs can be added in the empty middle. Write $F_{\mathrm{ht}}(n)$ for the maximum size when the chosen set must also be unchanged by a half-turn. For infinitely many primes $p\equiv1\pmod{840}$ we prove that $F_{\mathrm{ht}}(4p+1) \ge 6p+2+\exp(\log p/(114\log\log p))$. At side $n=4p+1$ this gives a super-polylogarithmic excess over $3n/2$: the guaranteed excess grows faster than every fixed power of $\log n$ along these sides. It does not raise the leading coefficient $3/2$. Code and certificates are available at https://github.com/dimkadimon/No3InLine
