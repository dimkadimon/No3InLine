# Before posting the no-three-in-line preprint

**Assessment, 19 November 2026.** The most defensible paper is the specific boundary augmentation of a credited four-period construction, together with the stronger symmetric interpolation theorem. There is a case for a short math.CO preprint after independent mathematical review. This is not evidence that a journal has accepted anything or that arXiv will announce it. No submission, author claim, endorsement request, or message to another researcher has been made.

| Claim | What to say |
|---|---|
| Unrestricted \((3/2-o(1))n\) for every large side | Hall–Jackson–Sudbery–Wild, JCTA (1975), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165(75)90043-6). See [Kovács–Nagy–Szabó, Theorems 1.2 and 2.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.07632v2). Not new. |
| Four-period ring, six points per occupied fibre, checkerboard and signed-coset colouring | [rStation 38, Theorems 1–2](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/38), with empirical mask credit to [paper 35, §5.4](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/35). Direct prior work, public even though not a journal article. Even the half-turn leading asymptotic is not the point of the submission: the old masks on the right odd/even board, or symmetric cropping, followed by embedding already explain it. |
| Eight off-carrier boundary points at every specified prime; positive fraction of eligible levels; rectangular repairs with 0/8/10 maximum loss and a square-root relocation | Precise results proved in the draft. Not stated in the above sources as far as checked. We have not excluded an independent discovery, an equivalent old construction in other coordinates, or an old lower bound already implying the unrestricted numerical inequality. Market the construction and the symmetry constraint, not a purported first crossing in all of mathematics. |
| Logarithmic advantage over the largest-prime-below rule | A comparison against a *specific rule*, not against the best construction. The appendix gives positive density for the specified anchored family. **Even classical HJSW alone**, by cropping a board at the *upper* prime of a long gap, gives a logarithmic improvement over that rule on a positive fraction of sides. Do not sell the unqualified numerical comparison as new. |
| Four larger finite packings | Exact coordinates, ordinary-line checks, and exact optima when the indicated seed is kept point-for-point and extra sites must preserve the half-turn. These do not settle the board optima. [rStation 47, §8](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/47) already used finite additions to dense seeds. |
| Earlier small-board census and catalogue additions | Different results, best assessed separately. The 13 encoded classes were compared to one dated Flammenkamp download; *absent from a snapshot* does not mean *first discovered*. Compare other work, including [Kudriashov's half-turn defect archive](https://zenodo.org/records/23939789). Our earlier order-36 candidate is close to a public configuration; comparing point sets to the three order-36 entries of that archive's appendix over all eight square symmetries gave minimum symmetric differences 128, 124, 8—not equality. This is only a comparison to those three entries. |

Other scope checks: [Kudriashov's two-period hyperbola note](https://zenodo.org/records/22275500) is about subsets of a carrier, not guaranteed off-carrier additions. [rStation 56](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/56) and [57](https://rstation.ai/problems/no_three_in_line/papers/57) are upper bounds on a single hyperbola; paper 57 explicitly does not give a construction. Sopin's 2023 [EasyChair preprint, v2](https://easychair.org/publications/preprint/7Pxv/open) mentions a better lower bound in the abstract; its displayed statistical model and remarks do not identify the specific bounds or witnesses here. Source reading is not worldwide priority clearance.

## Human review and self-submission

1. Have a combinatorial geometer check (i) the mixed old/new ray cases including the three exceptional primes, (ii) the signed-cycle incidences and small templates, (iii) the mixed-parity patch and the lattice lemma. Have a number theorist check the level-counting and optional sieve appendix. Repeat the companion verification independently; an independent implementation or machine would be preferable to merely reading the PASS logs.
2. Ask a knowledgeable researcher whether the **exact statement** at side \(4p+1\) or a stronger older construction is known. The [Flammenkamp catalogue](https://wwwhomes.uni-bielefeld.de/achim/no3in/readme.html) is especially relevant to claims about finite configurations. Do not ask an arXiv moderator for pre-clearance or send a bulk endorsement request.
3. Establish bona fide human authorship and agree on the final text. The draft is intentionally unsigned and already discloses substantial AI use for construction, proof development, code, experiments and drafting. Do not use a nominal author who cannot take responsibility for those contents. arXiv's [moderation and AI policy](https://info.arxiv.org/help/moderation/index.html) says significant use should be reported, human authors take responsibility for all contents, and AI should not be listed as an author.
4. Submit through the responsible author's registered account, normally to **math.CO (Combinatorics)**. Check [endorsement](https://info.arxiv.org/help/endorsement.html) for a first submission or a new category. Select a distribution license, review the submittal agreement, and use the [official submission workflow](https://info.arxiv.org/help/submit/index.html). The prepared source ZIP has `main.tex` at the root, the actual figure `ring.pdf`, and a separate `anc/` containing the code and finite certificates. Replace the unsigned draft author line only after review, rebuild and inspect the PDF generated on arXiv. See the [TeX instructions](https://info.arxiv.org/help/submit_tex.html) and [ancillary-file instructions](https://info.arxiv.org/help/ancillary_files.html). Moderation is not peer review.

**Next mathematical target:** prove that on some unbounded prime sequence the frozen or slightly modified ring admits an unbounded net number of mutually compatible extra sites beyond the eight. The four exact finite optima motivate this; they do not provide the required lower bound for infinitely many primes. Counting individually unblocked sites alone is not enough: pair and triple conflicts among the new sites must also be controlled.
