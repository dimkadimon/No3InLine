## Mathematical points raised in §2.2 of the report

| Point | Where to check it in R2 | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Nonparallel chords at one root | §5.2, Lemma 5.2 and Proposition 5.4 | A four-row table shows the cancelled divisor and the four remaining signed products. Small products have length `4p/ℓ`; signed factorization gives their divisor weights. A permutation of the nonzero residues and two applications of Cauchy–Schwarz lead to `O(p³L⁵/ℓ²)` per row and divisor. The two `DD₁` regimes then count the small-gcd pairs separately. Product equality plus nonparallel linear equations yields at most one outcome out of `p³` per pair. |
| 2. A ratio in an integer rectangle | Lemma 5.3 | Primitive integer pairs give at most two signed vectors per rational direction. Distinct directions have determinant at least `p` in absolute value. The proof separates `2ST<p`, `p/2≤ST<p` and the general angular packing bound; rescaling by each actual gcd gives `O(G+ST/p)`. In particular `ST<p` alone does **not** force a single direction. |
| 3. Parallel physical lifts | §5.1, Lemma 5.1 | The second chord's columns are aligned by residue. Factoring a sum of two rank-one matrices proves the common-column/common-row alternative for rank-one `E`. For invertible `E` the determinant gives `d | det E`. The primitive step for each affine lattice line is bounded from below on fixing either factor; summing by norm in the short box gives `O(p)` per bin, uniformly over `E,d`. |
| 4. Distinct roots, shared level | §6, Proposition 6.1 | Eliminating the level gives a linear equation for the first abscissa. The exceptional case requires both coefficients to vanish: the ratio is unique and a nonzero quadratic invariant agrees. Choosing the second direction first or the second parameters first bounds the exceptional count by `O(D₁T₁ min(D₁,T₁))`. The proof accounts separately for second bins, first templates, at most `p−1` abscissas and the **`p⁵`**, not `p⁶`, outcomes. |
| 5. All ordinary triples | §3 (geometric probabilities) and Lemma 7.1 (ten-type table) | Every multiset of ring, anchor and target types is assigned an exclusion. The table distinguishes axes, a mate pair, two independent indices, and three independent indices. |
| 6. Occurrences and bits | §2, Lemma 2.1; §§4–7 | An occurrence is indexed by representative, unordered pair of **physical** sites, and chosen endpoint. A line with `r` candidate sites supplies `r choose 2` clauses. A reused site creates new occurrences. The two partner cosets can label the same bit. A reflected clause asks for the very same bit values and is not counted again in Hall's degree bound. |
| 7. Character shifts | §3, Lemma 3.1 | The 21 reduced positive proper fractions of height at most eight generate 86 signed slopes and 42 ratio shifts; `0` and `XY` complete the list. Coefficients distinguish the polynomials over the rationals and, outside finitely many primes, after reduction. The proof then separates accidental coincidences at a sampled root from coincidence as polynomials before applying the character bound. |

Proposition 7.2 combines these unconditional bounds by linearity of expectation and alteration, not by assuming independent sign tests at a shared level. Section 8 checks the hypotheses of Stewart's theorem, exact order, the two ranges of `j`, and the logarithmic loss in passing to the stated surplus. No finite diagnostic replaces one of these proofs.
